BUYER GUIDE VISUAL

01 / DEFINE YOUR PROJECT

Start with the buyer brief.

Before comparing quotations, align your internal team on the market, customer, product range and commercial target.

MARKET

Where will you sell?

Country or region, customer expectations, applicable product requirements, sales channel and seasonal timing.

CUSTOMER

Who will play it?

Beginner, recreational, junior, improving or experienced golfer. Player profile affects product mix and configuration.

POSITIONING

What price tier?

Entry, mid-range or premium positioning. Share a target retail or landed-cost range if you have one.

RANGE

Which products?

Individual clubs, coordinated sets, left-hand models, junior ranges or a broader private-label collection.

02 / COMPARE PRODUCT SPECIFICATIONS

Compare like with like.

A product name alone is not enough. Ask every supplier to quote against the same model specification and approved component list.

SPECIFICATIONWHAT TO CONFIRMWHY IT MATTERS
ClubheadMaterial, construction, weight, finish and head design.Defines product positioning, feel, appearance and cost.
Face & loftFace material / thickness where applicable, loft options and lie angle.Helps compare model configuration and intended player.
ShaftGraphite or steel, model, weight, flex and length.Important for player fit, consistency and final cost.
GripModel, material, size, color and logo options.Influences feel, fit and brand presentation.
Hand & set make-upRight / left hand, club count, club numbers and set contents.Prevents mismatched assortment and packing errors.
Branding & packLogo artwork, placement, color, headcover, labels and carton marks.Ensures the product is ready for your sales channel.

Always request a model-specific specification sheet. Values and available options must be confirmed for the exact product and order.

VISUAL BUYER CHECKPOINTS

Check the people,
process and evidence.

A professional supplier should be able to show development references, production environments and quality checkpoints that match the project discussion.

Development

Does the supplier understand the brief?

Ask for drawings, engineering references, prototype discussions and examples of how product requirements are documented.

Production

Can the supplier show the production environment?

Current production-floor and process photographs help you understand whether the supplier can coordinate the manufacturing route you need.

Quality

What inspection evidence is available?

Review measurement tools, QC checkpoints and approval logic rather than relying on generic quality claims alone.

03 / VALIDATE THE SUPPLIER

Ask for evidence,
not just promises.

Use the same due-diligence questions with each shortlisted supplier so you can make a fair comparison.

01

Factory & process

Which processes are in-house? Which are outsourced? Can the supplier show current facility photos, equipment and relevant production stages?

02

Quality controls

What is checked at incoming, assembly and final stages? Which tolerances and acceptance criteria will be recorded?

03

Sample approval

Can you evaluate a sample? How are approved sample details transferred into the production order?

04

Business reliability

Confirm legal company details, communication contacts, order responsibilities, payment terms and issue-resolution process.

04 / CONFIRM COMMERCIAL TERMS

Put the details
in writing.

Do not compare unit prices without checking what is included. A complete quotation should make scope and assumptions visible.

MOQ & price basis

Minimum order quantity by model, configuration, logo and packaging; EXW / FOB / CIF basis and currency.

Sample & tooling

Sample price, sample lead time, tooling or setup charges if applicable, and whether any fee is refundable.

Production schedule

Lead time after specification and sample approval, deposit, artwork and component confirmation.

Payment & shipment

Payment milestones, shipping marks, carton details, destination, Incoterms and required export documents.

MOQ, price, tooling charges, sample terms, lead time, payment terms and Incoterms are not universal. Confirm the final terms in a written quotation and purchase agreement.

05 / FREQUENT BUYER QUESTIONS

Questions to ask
before the first order.

Can I start with samples before placing a bulk order?

Ask which models can be sampled, the sample charge, available configurations and estimated sample lead time. Confirm whether the approved sample will be retained as the production reference.

Can you make golf clubs under my own brand?

Share your logo artwork, placement preferences, product category and packaging direction. The available customization scope, MOQ and any setup charges should be confirmed for the selected model.

What is the minimum order quantity?

MOQ can vary by product, components, logo application, packaging and whether the order is a standard configuration or a custom development. Request a model-specific quotation rather than relying on a general figure.

How long does production take?

Lead time depends on product availability, component sourcing, customization, sample approval and order quantity. Ask for a schedule with clear start conditions and milestones.

Can you provide inspection reports or certifications?

Specify the exact test, standard or document you need. Ask for the actual report, issuing organization, product scope and validity. Do not assume a certificate covers every model or market.

Which shipping terms can I use?

Tell the supplier your destination port or delivery address and ask for the quotation basis. Confirm Incoterms, freight responsibility, insurance, customs responsibilities and required documents.
